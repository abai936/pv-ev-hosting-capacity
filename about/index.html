About this project

I am Aniket Bailwal, an Electrical and Electronic Engineering student at the University of Auckland. This is one simulation project, at version 0.9, about how much rooftop solar and how many EV chargers a 55-house NZ-style low-voltage street can take.

How this was built

I designed and directed this using generative AI tools: the code was written with AI coding assistants, and every result went through tests and an independent review.

What I decided: the question, the scope and the assumptions. What I reviewed: the results and the checks listed with their limits on the Method and checks page. The tests and hand checks were drafted with AI coding assistants and run by them.

I am working through the hand checks myself.

An independent review step checked the results as well.

What I would do next

My judgement, not a result:

  • Get a real rating source from an NZ distribution engineer.
  • A 1-minute worst-week re-run to measure what 30-minute steps hide.
  • A rating sweep around the assumed value, to show how much the results depend on it.
  • A second feeder.

Load and EV profiles are derived from Steidl, Zabala Figueroa et al., "High-resolution residential energy load profiles under varying electrification scenarios for NZ and Germany using resLoadSIM", Zenodo v1, 8 May 2025, https://zenodo.org/records/15362256 (linked below), licensed CC BY 4.0. The data are synthetic and NZ-parameterised. Aggregated to 30 minutes and scaled; changes were made by the author.

Feeder layout (hero drawing): IEEE PES European LV Test Feeder, simplified redraw from the bus coordinates; no licence stated; not an IEEE figure.
