# R4: NZ-style data sources for a simulation-only PV + EV hosting-capacity project

Date read: 2026-11-18. Method: WebSearch / WebFetch only, about 24 calls, read-only. No downloads, logins or bulk fetches. Two PDFs (EECA, Steidl paper) were auto-saved by the fetch tool and read locally as text, so their statements are VERIFIED from the primary document. Nothing here was downloaded as a dataset.

## Summary

- There is NO openly licensed measured NZ half-hourly residential/LV load dataset that I could confirm. EMI data is wholesale/GXP-level with all-rights-reserved site copyright; GREEN Grid (measured, about 40 households per region, 2014-15) is gated behind UK Data Service registration.
- Best open NZ-specific load + EV source: Steidl et al. (2025) Zenodo dataset, CC BY 4.0. It is SYNTHETIC (resLoadSIM), 1-minute, full year (2022 weather), Auckland and Christchurch, with EV scenarios (reference, 38%, 100% EV). Must be labelled synthetic. About 989 MB zip, so downloading is a separate decision.
- Solar for Auckland: no confirmed free NZ TMY file. Realistic options are PVGIS-ERA5 (hourly, about 0.28 degree, NZ coverage not confirmed), NASA POWER (NZ hourly coverage not confirmed by me) or Open-Meteo Himawari satellite radiation (covers NZ, 10-min native, free tier non-commercial only). EECA/AMCL Appendix 7 gives published NZ PV capacity factors for Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch, Queenstown as a sanity check (the underlying ANSA TMY series is not published).
- EV charging behaviour: EECA Feb 2023 survey (n=712, self-selected) gives start-time distribution (34% start at 9pm, 61% start after 9pm, off-peak driven). Vector Auckland trial (about 200 EVs) gives about 3-4 h active charging per session and median 6.9 kWh per session (from search snippets only). No open session-level data found.
- EV fleet/penetration: government target 30% of light fleet ZEV by 2035; Transpower 2020 report projected about 1.5 million light EVs in 2035; light EV fleet about 113,715 in 2024 (EHINZ). These come from search snippets or secondary sources, so INFERRED until the primary pages are read.
- Realistic student path: Steidl synthetic Auckland profiles (aggregate to 30 min) + PVGIS/NASA POWER/Open-Meteo irradiance for Auckland through a simple PV model + EV sessions built from EECA/Vector parameters. Label the whole pipeline "synthetic, NZ-parameterised".

If neither source gives NZ coverage when tried, fall back to a clearly labelled synthetic clear-sky + cloud-factor profile.
- EV: build sessions synthetically. README label: "EV charging sessions are synthetic, parameterised from EECA (2023 survey start-time distribution) and Vector trial summary statistics (median 6.9 kWh, 3-4 h); charger sizes 7.4 kW single-phase and 11 kW three-phase are assumptions, not NZ-verified."
- Penetration scenarios (labelled as scenarios, not forecasts): 0%, about 10-30% (near current), 30% (government 2035 target), about 100% worst case, with a "peak-coincident 9pm start" stress case from the EECA 34% finding.

## Assessment: realistic combination for a year-long 30 min or 1 h series

1. Load: Steidl 1-min Auckland CSV, averaged to 30 min. This gives a full year with seasonality and EV scenarios already included. It is the only free, NZ-specific, year-long option I found.
2. PV: hourly Auckland irradiance for a single year from PVGIS-ERA5 (check NZ coverage by actually querying), else Open-Meteo Himawari (check licence and archive start), else NASA POWER. Interpolate to 30 min if needed. Use the same year as the load (2022) if the source allows, so weather correlation with the load is consistent.
3. EV: synthetic sessions on top (or reuse Steidl's EV profiles), with 9pm-start-heavy distribution.
4. Second city: Christchurch is available in the Steidl data and EECA Appendix 7 for cross-checking; this is the easy second city.
5. Biggest caveats to state: all load and EV data are synthetic; EECA survey is a self-selected sample; fleet forecasts from search snippets must be confirmed against primary pages before being cited.
