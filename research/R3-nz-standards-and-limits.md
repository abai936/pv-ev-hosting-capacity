# R3: NZ standards and limits for a solar + EV hosting-capacity study

Date of research: 2026-11-18 (all "Date read" entries below are this date). Tool-limited: WebSearch/WebFetch only, no paywalled downloads.
Method note: several PDFs could not be read by the fetch tool directly; the tool saved the free public PDF and I extracted its text locally with pdftotext. legislation.govt.nz returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool, so the legislation text itself was NOT read; the 2025 regulation change is confirmed through the Electricity Authority (EA) and MBIE documents that describe it (see rows).

## Summary

- CORRECTION to your assumption: the old NZ limit was 230 V symmetric +/-6% (not +10/-6). Since 13 Nov 2025 the Electricity (Safety) Regulations 2010 reg 28 is 230 V +/-10%, i.e. 207 V to 253 V. +10/-6 was only an MBIE consultation option that was not adopted. Use 253 V / 207 V as limits in OpenDSS.
- Since 11 May 2026 the Code (Part 6, cl 6.3B) says distributors must require LV inverters to use the "Australia A" settings of AS/NZS 4777.2:2020 (incl. Amendments 1 and 2) from 11 Sep 2026, unless a network study justifies otherwise. So there is no separate NZ volt-var/volt-watt curve in force; the numbers are the Australia A ones. Vector (May 2026) and Jemena (Australia) both publish them free (volt-watt 253 V/100% to 260 V/20%; volt-var 207/220/240/258 V with +44% / 0 / 0 / -60% Q).
- The standard's own text, Region B/C and old "NZ region" volt-var/volt-watt tables, and the 2015 numeric tables are paywalled and not found free. The only free "old NZ setting" number found is sustained-overvoltage disconnection at 249 V (EA decision paper).
- Part 6 thresholds you can cite: 10 kW or less (Part 1 / Part 1A), above 10 kW (Part 2); default Part 1A export limit 10 kW (cl 6.3A); distributors cannot cap nameplate capacity for Part 1/1A. 30 kW appears only as an Orion policy scope threshold, not in the Code.
- Best NZ hosting-capacity method to mirror: Orion's published Congestion Management Policy (1 Apr 2026): +20 percentage points PV penetration scenario, DG placed by network impedance, export stepped in 1 kW/phase increments up to 10 kW/phase until a voltage or thermal limit, results set static export limits. Academic: Watson et al. 2016 (Canterbury, 10,558 LV networks).
