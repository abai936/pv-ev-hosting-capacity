Method and checks

What I checked, and what I did not.

Everything on this site is simulated: OpenDSS on the IEEE European LV Test Feeder, synthetic NZ-parameterised load, modelled solar, assumed cable ratings. Nothing is measured.

The checks ladder

Each rung says what was compared, the result, and what it does not prove. The status word is written out and has its own shape, so it does not depend on colour: a filled square means an independent source, a half circle means a hand calculation, a dashed square means no independent check.

Test run, with its limits

Many tests check the code against its own inputs. Test counts are not a measure of how close the model is to a real network.

Source: STATE.md.

What is NOT checked

  • PV, EV, inverter and rating results have no independent source.
  • The energy balances only prove signs and units.
  • No comparison with a real network or with measured data.
  • The inverter curve points come from free summaries, not the paywalled AS/NZS 4777.2. Var priority (watts cut first) is an assumption with no source.
  • The feeder file has no cable ratings. Ratings are assumed from datasheets matched by cable size, so thermal results are relative, not absolute.
  • This work does not implement the Electricity Authority's new export limit assessment method.

Worked examples

The maths, in short

Worked examples, written with AI assistance; I am working through these checks myself. Every formula and number below is already in the project files, with its source beside it. They are simulated and hand-calculated values, not measurements.

Voltage rise at the far end

ΔV=Rpath I,I=PV\Delta V = R_{\text{path}}\, I, \qquad I = \frac{P}{V}ΔV=Rpath​I,I=VP​

For 5 kW at the far-end house: 4.787218 V by hand and 4.786654 V in OpenDSS. Here RpathR_{\text{path}}Rpath​ is the real part of the cable impedance summed along the path to that house.

Source: results/stage4_hand_check.csv

No-load voltage

V=4163 puV = \frac{416}{\sqrt{3}}\, \mathrm{pu}V=3​416​pu

At 1.00 pu this gives 240.1777 V; at 1.05 pu it gives 252.1866 V. Hand value and OpenDSS value agree.

Cable-limit hosting capacity (the Stage 4 load case)

HC=Irating V+PlocalnhousesHC = \frac{I_{\text{rating}}\, V + P_{\text{local}}}{n_{\text{houses}}}HC=nhouses​Irating​V+Plocal​​

Worked example at 100% penetration: (195 A x 245 V + 12.8 kW local load) / 21 houses on that phase = 2.88 kW by hand, against 2.91 kW from OpenDSS. The 195 A rating is an assumption.

Bisection: how the search narrows

n≥log⁡21 kW0.01 kW=log⁡2100≈6.64  ⇒  n=7,27=128>100n \ge \log_2\frac{1\ \text{kW}}{0.01\ \text{kW}} = \log_2 100 \approx 6.64 \;\Rightarrow\; n = 7, \quad 2^{7} = 128 > 100n≥log2​0.01 kW1 kW​=log2​100≈6.64⇒n=7,27=128>100

Power is raised in 1 kW steps until a limit breaks, then bisected to 0.01 kW. About 7 halvings are enough. The 7 and 128 are my own arithmetic on those two numbers.

Source: src/hc/hosting.py (module docstring)

Voltage unbalance factor

VUF=∣V2∣∣V1∣×100 %\mathrm{VUF} = \frac{|V_2|}{|V_1|} \times 100\,\%VUF=∣V1​∣∣V2​∣​×100%

In the code: VUF = |V2| / |V1| x 100, from the positive and negative sequence voltages at three-phase buses. A hand calculation gave 2.51 % for a known phasor set. The 2 % limit used later is an assumed European value, not an NZ limit.

Source: src/hc/feeder.py, function vuf_percent; VALIDATION.md row 10

Energy balance

Eload+Elosses−EPV=EtransformerE_{\text{load}} + E_{\text{losses}} - E_{\text{PV}} = E_{\text{transformer}}Eload​+Elosses​−EPV​=Etransformer​

In words: load + losses - PV = transformer energy. This is bookkeeping: it catches a missed element, not a wrong model.

Source: VALIDATION.md rows 24 to 26

Method in short

  • Network: IEEE European LV Test Feeder (50 Hz, 11 kV to 0.416 kV, 800 kVA, 907 buses, 55 customers). Its impedances and ratio are kept; voltage bases are set so 1 pu is 230 V. The source is 1.00 pu in the base case and is varied as an assumption.
  • Limits: 207 to 253 V at customers (NZ low-voltage limit since 13 Nov 2025), assumed cable ratings, the 800 kVA transformer.
  • Hosting capacity: kW per solar customer, on one phase, at stated penetrations, for a midday summer snapshot. It is similar in shape to Orion's published method, not identical. The year time series gives violation hours and curtailed energy.
  • Inverter settings: Australia A volt-var and volt-watt, from free summaries.

Data sources and attribution

The data are synthetic and NZ-parameterised. No feeder file or raw data are published on this site: only results and figures. The download script and DATA.md in the project files list the sources.

  • Load and EV profiles: Steidl, Zabala Figueroa et al., "High-resolution residential energy load profiles under varying electrification scenarios for NZ and Germany using resLoadSIM", Zenodo v1, 8 May 2025, https://zenodo.org/records/15362256, licensed CC BY 4.0. Aggregated to 30 minutes and scaled; changes were made by the author.
  • Feeder layout (hero drawing): IEEE PES European LV Test Feeder, simplified redraw from the bus coordinates; no licence stated; not an IEEE figure.
  • Solar: modelled with PVGIS (licence not checked).
  • Cable ratings: from third-party datasheets, described in research/R10-line-ratings.md. The ratings file is not published.
  • Uptake forecast: EA solar data and Ministry of Transport EVs via Figure.NZ, with Stats NZ and MBIE. Licences for some of these are not checked, so only derived result figures are shown.
